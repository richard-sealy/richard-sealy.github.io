What the mountains teach

Alongside my coaching practice I am the operational lead for the Mountain Adventure Fund, a charitable initiative that brings children to the mountains of Snowdonia, many of them for the first time. We are part of the Mountain Training Trust, which runs the National Outdoor Centre at Plas y Brenin. Our aim is simple. Every child, whatever their background, should have the chance to be inspired by wild places and to test themselves against them.

Watching a group arrive is instructive. Some children step off the bus already restless to get up the hill. Others look at the weather and the ridgeline and would clearly rather be anywhere else. What happens over the following days rarely has much to do with fitness or natural ability. It has to do with what each child believes about themselves, and how quickly that belief starts to shift once they are given something genuinely difficult to do and someone steady alongside them while they do it.

By the last day the transformation can be striking. A child who refused to leave the minibus is standing on a summit in the wind, tired and soaked and visibly taller. Nothing about their circumstances has changed. What changed is the evidence they now hold about what they can manage.

I think about this often in my coaching work, because senior executives are not so different. Confidence in adults is built the same way it is built on a hillside, through doing hard things with the right support and then noticing that you did them. It is rarely built through reassurance alone. A coach, like a good mountain instructor, is not there to carry anyone up the hill. The job is to choose the right hill, keep an eye on the weather and stay close enough that the person doing the climbing feels able to keep going.

The children we work with would laugh at the comparison, and fair enough. Their hills are real and the rain in Snowdonia is very real indeed. But the lesson they hand back to me every visit is one I try to bring into every coaching room. People are almost always capable of more than the story they arrived with.
